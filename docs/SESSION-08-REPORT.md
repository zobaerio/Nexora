# NEXORA — Session 08 Report: Weather, Climate, Environment & Ecosystem Intelligence Engine

**Status: COMPLETE** — the environmental layer was built by EXTENDING Sessions 01–07 (never recreating a system): the engine's existing daily weather draw, Session 03's terrain/infrastructure/buildings/roads, Session 04's market and goods, Session 05's law book and treasuries, Session 06's needs/health state and Session 07's trips and emissions statistics all stay exactly where they were; this session adds the regional, hydrological and ecological machinery around them. Two new modules (`tools/sim/environment.py`, `tools/sim/environmentview.py`), one new config file (`data/environment-config.json`), additive edits to seven existing files. **All conditions in this layer are SIMULATED — never real-world observations, never scientific forecasts.**

## What was built

- **Regional weather engine** — Rupsha City's daily preset remains the engine's own `daily_weather` draw, byte-for-byte the behaviour Sessions 01–07 were tested against. Shibnagar Town now evolves its own preset by a seeded Markov-style reweighting of the same preset weights: the season's rain factor and the region's rain factor scale the wet presets, and yesterday's wet/dry state persists (wet ×1.6). Every region's full daily record — temperature day/night, humidity, pressure, wind, cloud, precipitation mm, visibility, sunrise/sunset — is derived from the preset's own data in `weather-presets.json` plus the documented season/region/climate offsets, persisted in `env_weather` (a restart cannot re-roll it).
- **Seasons + day/night** — the six-season Bangladeshi calendar (Sheet/Boshonto/Grishmo/Borsha/Shorot/Hemonto) as config data tiling the 365-day sim year exactly once (validate_data-guarded). Seasons move temperature offsets, rain factors and daylight: sunrise/sunset are the documented approximation (06:00/18:00 shifted by the season's amplitude, −55 min winter … +35 min summer) recorded on every weather row.
- **Water systems** — one catchment per city over Session 03's terrain: daily rainfall is drained first by Session 03's real drainage network capacity, the rest runs off to the river; infiltration recharges groundwater, residents draw it down; surface storage fills and evaporates; the river rises with runoff and recedes toward its base level at 15%/day. Water availability is computed monthly per city and snapshotted.
- **Flooding (a mechanism, not a scenario)** — when the river crosses the configured flood level (3.4 m), the exposed ground floods. Exposure is geographic, from Session 03 terrain: parcels whose centre sits in a floodplain/wetland region, zones whose centre does. During an episode: road segments with an endpoint in an exposed zone are closed (`road_segments.closed` — Session 07 routing detours from the next day), exposed buildings lose 4 condition/day (floored at 25), businesses in them record an `environment_disruption` event, households resident in exposed zones are displaced by record, and exposed residents take a measured needs impact (healthcare −2, safety −2). The episode ends when the river falls 0.5 m below the flood level; recovery then repairs the recorded damage from the treasury (below).
- **Agriculture** — crop cycles on the real agricultural parcels (3 in Shibnagar, the only farmland that exists): aman paddy plants in monsoon, boro paddy in winter, winter vegetables in late autumn. Growth is scored monthly from the parcel's soil moisture, soil fertility and the city's recorded rainfall; at maturity a crop at/above the 0.35 threshold harvests — its lots enter Session 04's market through `adjust_inventory` (movement kind `harvest`) on the city's agriculture business, so farm economics flow through the existing market/ledger machinery — and below it, fails by record and yields nothing. No crop price was invented: `agricultural_produce` and its price are Session 04's own.
- **Soil** — per-parcel moisture (rain-fed, daily loss) and fertility (drained by cropping, recovered in fallow, eroded by flood episodes), all SIM MODEL and labelled.
- **Pollution with traceable sources** — monthly per-city air/water indices (0–100) whose every point is accounted in a stored source breakdown: air = Session 07's recorded vehicle emissions (transport_statistics, kg ÷ 900 per point) + active industry (manufacturing/food-processing businesses × 2); water = Session 03 wastewater/drainage overload share × 30 + flood episodes × 12. A city whose air index passes 45 applies a measured healthcare-need impact to its residents that month.
- **Disasters — configured scenarios only** — six scenario definitions in config (cyclone, flood surge, two droughts, heatwave, severe storm); the standard world arms **none** of them, and the only door is `trigger_scenario()` with a configured id (unknown ids raise). Each event records id/type/city/intensity/duration/affected population/damage estimate/relief and repair totals. Daily effects are real: cyclone/severe storm damage exposed buildings, close exposed roads and displace households; flood surge forces the river up into the flood mechanism; drought scales the affected city's precipitation (×(1 − 0.8 × intensity)) in the weather computation itself; heatwave adds its temperature offset and a daily measured needs impact.
- **Response with actual resources only** — when a disaster leaves its active phase: relief of 3,000 NCr per displaced household is paid from the city's municipal treasury (Session 05) — or SYS-CITY without a government layer — through the ledger with `transfer_key` + `event_ref` = the disaster id, capped by the treasury's real balance and a 400,000 NCr monthly cap; shelter is assigned to a real civic-owned building in a safe zone (recorded as *unavailable* when none exists — never invented); one evacuation trip per displaced household head is recorded in Session 07's `trips` table (walk, purpose `evacuation`, shelter zone, disaster id in details); displaced residents are flagged for the Session 06 clinic queue via their existing health profiles. Recovery repairs recorded building damage at 2 condition points per 1,000 NCr from the same treasury (20,000 NCr/day cap) until nothing remains damaged — only then does the disaster close as `recovered`, with roads reopened.
- **Environmental governance** — Session 05's law book gains a fifth hook: the `emission_limit` law kind (justice.py `LAW_KINDS` + constitutional bounds 0–60% in government-config.json, debatable/enactable through the existing machinery on the parties' `development` stance). `environment.effective_emission_reduction` reads the law book exactly as justice.py's own hooks do: an enacted law reduces the industry component of the air index by its percent from its effective month and adds a conservation bonus to vegetation recovery. Test-proven both ways (identical worlds, law vs no law).
- **Vegetation + wildlife (abstracted indices)** — cover index per habitat region (wetland/forest/river terrain + park land), growing toward its class ceiling in wet months, declining under drought and recorded construction, recovering faster under an enacted conservation law. Wildlife is a population *index* per habitat region drifting toward vegetation cover × water availability — labelled in config and code as an abstracted index, not a biological claim.
- **Long-term trends** — monthly `env_snapshots` per city: average temperature, total rainfall, river/groundwater/storage end-state, water availability index, vegetation cover, air/water indices, exposed-resident vulnerability share, and the active climate scenario's name. Climate scenarios (baseline / warming_drier / warming_wetter) are config deltas applied transparently and recorded on every snapshot.
- **Effects on people** — flood exposure and heatwaves move real needs state; polluted air moves healthcare need; water availability and agricultural employment ride the existing systems (Session 03 infrastructure already degrades needs on water overload; farm harvests feed Session 04's food chain). No parallel human system was built.
- **Observatory** — `--environment` CLI + JSON (`environmentview.py`): recent regional weather, water state, exposed zones, climate trend snapshots, pollution with source breakdowns, vegetation/wildlife, soil, disaster records with impact/response counts, crop outcomes, and the emission-law state. Data layer only; the view carries the simulated-not-observed note on its face.

## Limitations (honest)

- **Monsoon rainfall totals run high** (Shibnagar's month-7 total in the scenario run was 1,135 mm): the preset-rate × season-factor product is a labelled SIM MODEL, tuned for mechanism response, not calibrated to meteorological records. No claim of realism attaches to the absolute totals.
- **Rupsha City has no farmland** — all agriculture happens on Shibnagar's 3 parcels, because those are the only agricultural parcels in the merged data. District 1's food still arrives through Session 04's existing production/trade machinery.
- **Disaster response is deliberately resource-bound**: when a municipal treasury is empty, relief pays 0 and repairs stall (the disaster stays in `responding`). Two of the scenario run's flood episodes were still responding at day 210 for exactly this reason. That is the spec's "actual resources" rule operating, not a hidden failure — but it means recovery timelines depend on Session 05's fiscal state.
- **Earthquake, landslide, wildfire and tsunami exist only as scenario *types* the config format can express** — no scenario definitions are authored for them, because Rupsha/Shibnagar's terrain data (riverine delta) gives no honest exposure basis for them. Cyclone/flood/drought/heatwave/severe-storm are the authored set.
- **Wildlife and vegetation are indices, not ecology**: single cover/population numbers per habitat region, drifting by documented rules. They exist for Session 10 aggregation and make no species-level claim.
- **Snow/hail never occur**: no preset in this climate's data produces them (snow stays zone-gated off in weather-presets.json), and the environment layer does not invent them.
- **Flood exposure is zone/parcel-centre geographic**, not hydraulic: a building at a floodplain parcel's high edge is treated as exposed. Finer elevation-based exposure would need per-parcel elevation data the merged files do not carry.
